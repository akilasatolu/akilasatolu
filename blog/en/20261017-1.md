# I Moved Review Back to a Subagent, and Tried Haiku for the Worker but Stayed With Sonnet

In [my previous post](20261011-2.md), I wrote that I had changed my setup so that the main agent (Opus) handles both PM and review, and leaves research, design, and development to Sonnet subagents. I've since reviewed how I work again, so here's a summary.

To start with the conclusion: I decided to hand review off to a subagent. I also tried to see whether Haiku could replace the worker for research, design, and development, but I'm keeping the worker on Sonnet.

Here is the flow of my platform as it stands now.

![Flow diagram of the platform. The user talks to the PM (main agent, Opus). The PM instructs the worker (subagent, Sonnet, doing research, design, and development) and the reviewer (subagent, Opus).](../images/20261017-1-1.png)

## Why I Handed Review Back to a Subagent

Having the main agent do both PM and review had two problems.

- **The review step makes the main agent's context grow.** Every time it read and checked a deliverable, the information the main agent carried grew.
- **It can't review from an independent viewpoint.** The main agent knows the whole history, so bias creeps in no matter what.

So I decided to hand the review step to a subagent. That keeps the main agent's context from growing, and the review comes from a third party who doesn't know the history.

## Can Haiku Replace Research, Design, and Development?

I wondered whether I could switch the worker from Sonnet to Haiku, so I tried it.

I used a copy of an existing project, actually ran three tasks (small, medium, and large), and compared the results.

| Aspect | Result |
| --- | --- |
| Speed | About even. Sonnet was faster on the small task |
| Tokens | About the same amount. Because of the price difference, Haiku costs about 1/20 as much |
| Accuracy | Sonnet was better. Haiku gave reports that did not match the facts, and the review missed one of them |

Haiku costs about 1/20 as much, but I'm on the Pro plan, so cost doesn't bother me much. Speed and token usage were the same, so there wasn't much to gain from switching to Haiku.

## Conclusion: The Worker Stays Sonnet

What decided it was whether the reports matched the facts.

The PM doesn't re-check the contents of the results; it relies on the worker's reports to keep things moving. So if a report doesn't match the facts, the work simply moves on to the next step. This time, the review missed one of Haiku's reports that didn't match the facts.

If I don't care about cost, there is little to gain, and only the risk of lower report accuracy remains. With that in mind, I kept the worker on Sonnet.

## Summary

- With the main agent doing both PM and review, the context grows and I can't get an independent viewpoint, so I decided to hand review to a subagent
- I compared Haiku as the worker on three tasks: small, medium, and large
- Speed was about even, tokens were about the same, and Haiku cost about 1/20 as much
- Sonnet was more accurate: Haiku gave reports that did not match the facts, and the review missed one of them
- Cost doesn't bother me on the Pro plan, and the PM relies on reports to keep things moving, so I put weight on reports matching the facts and kept the worker on Sonnet

Lowering cost means nothing if the reports can't be trusted. In my environment right now, staying with Sonnet felt the safest.
